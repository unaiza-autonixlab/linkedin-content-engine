# Distribution

Posting is one lever. This file covers the others, and the honest state of the
evidence behind each.

Researched 2026-07-21.

---

## The finding that should frame everything else

**Nobody measures inbound leads.**

Every dataset in this space measures impressions, engagement rate, and follower
growth. The chain from "2.6% engagement rate" to "a $3,500 deal in your inbox"
is asserted constantly and measured nowhere.

So treat everything below as proxy metrics, and treat any vendor claiming to
know what drives inbound as selling you something.

**And be sceptical of engagement statistics specifically.** Research checked one
widely cited "LinkedIn commenting statistics" page directly: of 16 statistics
presented, **2 cited a source**, and those cited a vendor blog. The numbers
circulating for the same relationship are mutually inconsistent across vendors
(5:1, 3x, and 15x for comment-vs-like weighting). That pattern is the signature
of invented numbers.

---

## 1. Commenting, roughly 30 minutes a day

**Ranked first, and not because the evidence is strong. It is not.**

The circulating statistics are fabricated. What is measurable:

- Van der Blom, via summary: engaging 15 to 30 minutes before and after you post
  improves reach "by up to 20%". Vendor summarised, not independently verified
- River Editor, 300 posts: author engagement in the **first 60 minutes** matters
  more than posting time
- Metricool, 673k posts: posts with a comment CTA average +80% comments. That
  measures getting comments on your own posts, not what commenting elsewhere
  does for you

**Why it still ranks first:** the mechanism does not need algorithm data to be
true. Commenting puts your name and headline in front of an audience someone
else already assembled. That is structural, and it is the best possible fit for
a small account with no distribution of its own, in a year where reach for
1,000 to 5,000 follower accounts is collapsing.

**No credible data exists on the right volume.** Practitioner consensus, which
is opinion:

- 5 to 10 substantive comments a day
- On posts from accounts whose audience **is** your ICP, not your peers
- Within the first hour of them publishing
- Two sentences or more, adding a specific claim or counter-example. Agreement
  adds nothing and gets read as filler

## 2. The profile is the conversion point, whether or not you designed it as one

Not a preference. A structural consequence of the link penalty.

External links cost **-27% impressions and -20% interactions** on personal
profiles (Metricool, 673k posts), with an independent smaller test finding
roughly **-60% reach** (River Editor). Company pages see the opposite, +51%.

So LinkedIn cannot be a click-to-landing-page channel for a solo profile.
Everything routes through the profile or a DM. The profile carries the
conversion for every impression you will ever earn, which makes it a fixed cost
worth paying once.

**Evidence status: none.** No data was found on headline formulas, banner
design, About structure, or Featured section impact. Everything published on
this is opinion. Treat profile work as high leverage because of the mechanism,
not because anyone has measured the tactics.

**One correction worth acting on: Creator Mode no longer exists.** LinkedIn
retired it around mid-2023 and folded its features into all profiles. Any advice
from 2023 or 2024 that tells you to toggle creator mode is stale, and so is
probably the rest of that article.

## 3. Format, then frequency

The best evidenced item in the whole research pass, because two independent
samples agree.

- Documents/carousels **7.00%** engagement, multi-image **6.90%**, text **3.95%**
  (Socialinsider, 1.3M posts)
- Multi-image **3.71%** vs single image **1.81%** (Metricool, independent sample)
- Video is the one format both agree is falling: views down 36% year on year

**Frequency: 2 to 3 posts a week, not more.** Van der Blom caps the useful range
at 1 to 3; River Editor at 3 to 4 and warns that daily posting without sustained
engagement triggers suppression. Both point the same way.

**Posting more is not the lever.** If time is short, cut posts before cutting
comment replies.

---

## 4. Recycling

- 50% of a post's impressions land in the **first 48 hours** (Metricool)
- Most reach is determined in the **first 90 minutes** (van der Blom)
- **Post reactivation:** commenting on your own post after 8 or 24 hours pushes
  it back into feeds. Recommended tactic, not a measured result
- No source found any penalty for republishing old material. Rewriting a strong
  post as a new post weeks later appears unpenalised. The risk is audience
  fatigue, not the algorithm

**Retracted:** "LinkedIn resurfaces posts 2 to 3 weeks later" has no supporting
dataset and the measured picture contradicts it.

## 5. Measurement, and why not to buy a tool

**Shield has shut down**, and its founders state plainly: "Both Google and
LinkedIn made it clear that we could not continue operating Shield as it was
built." No successor is recommended.

Shield was the best known and best funded player in the category, and platform
pressure ended it. Anything filling that gap inherits both the discontinuation
risk and the risk to the account you connect to it.

Since the content system and the lead gen work both depend on account health, do
not connect the primary account to a scraper-backed analytics tool.

**What to track, from native analytics only:**

- **Profile views** and **search appearances**. These are the only metrics that
  measure someone *deciding to look into you*, which is the closest available
  proxy for intent. This is reasoning, not evidence
- **Saves.** Worth roughly 5x a like in reach
- **DMs and inbound.** The only metric that pays

Engagement rate is a poor proxy here. Published averages span all account types
and say nothing about buyer intent.

---

## 6. Timing: stop optimising it

Four datasets, four incompatible answers. Buffer's 4.8M-post sample says Tuesday
is among the worst days; van der Blom says it is the best. One source lists
12-2pm as optimal, another says specifically avoid it.

Three of the four agree on one thing: your own engagement in the first 60 to 90
minutes matters more than the clock. Pick a slot you can reliably be online for
and hold it.

---

## 7. Not evaluated

- **LinkedIn newsletters.** Current mechanics could not be verified; the one
  retrievable source was stale enough to still reference Creator Mode. One
  durable claim worth checking: links are reportedly *not* downranked inside
  newsletters, which given the link penalty would be the main strategic reason
  to use one. Also note LinkedIn owns the subscriber list and you never get the
  email addresses, which is a real cost for a $3,500 offer
- **DM handling.** No study found. Left blank rather than filled with the same
  unsourced material found elsewhere

## Ranked by impact per hour, for a one hour daily budget

This ranking is judgement. The evidence base does not support a data-driven
ranking of lead generation levers, because nobody has measured it.

1. **Comment on ICP-adjacent accounts, ~30 min/day.** Only lever whose mechanism
   does not depend on already having distribution
2. **Fix the profile once, 2 to 3 hours, then leave it.** Forced to be the
   conversion point by the link penalty. Amortised across every future impression
3. **2 to 3 posts a week, documents or multi-image, no external links,
   ~20 min/day.** Best evidenced item here. Do not spend the saved time posting more

**Explicitly not in the top three:** timing optimisation, newsletters, and
anything built on a third-party analytics tool.
